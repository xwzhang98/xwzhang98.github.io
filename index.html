I am a Ph.D. candidate in Physics at Carnegie Mellon University (expected 2027), working at the McWilliams Center for Cosmology & Astrophysics with Prof. Tiziana Di Matteo and Prof. Rupert Croft. I build machine learning methods that make expensive scientific computation reachable.

My research develops AI methods that break the volume–resolution trade-off in cosmological simulation:

  • Super-resolution and emulation — a time-conditioned style-based GAN that super-resolves N-body simulations by 512× in particle count, a deterministic emulator that recovers specific high-resolution realizations, and a hydrodynamic field emulator reaching sub-percent accuracy at ~450× the speed of direct simulation.
  • Foundation models for simulations — a shared embedding space that makes simulations comparable across codes, resolutions, and modalities, plus interpretability of what astronomical foundation models like AION-1 encode physically.
  • LLM agents for research — SimAgents (IJCNLP-AACL 2025), a multi-agent system that turns papers and manuals into runnable simulation configurations.

I am the principal investigator on an ACCESS HPC allocation, and I release my methods as open-source software (map2map, SREmulator).

Before CMU, I received my B.S. in Physics from ShanghaiTech University, spent a year as a visiting student at UC Berkeley, and worked on topological data analysis at ShanghaiTech’s Institute of Mathematical Sciences.

selected publications

  1. MNRAS
    AI-assisted super-resolution cosmological simulations III: time evolution
    Xiaowen Zhang, Patrick Lachance, Yueying Ni, and 5 more authors
    Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society, Feb 2024
    Abs DOI arXiv HTML Code

    We extend our super-resolution (SR) model for cosmological simulations to produce fully time-consistent evolving representations of the particle phase-space distribution. We employ a style-based constrained generative adversarial network, where cosmic time is an input style parameter to the network. The matter power spectrum and halo mass function agree well with high-resolution N-body results over the full trained redshift range (10 >= z >= 0), and halo merger trees confirm the temporal consistency of the generated simulations.

  2. OJA
    AI-assisted super-resolution cosmological simulations IV: An emulator for deterministic realizations
    Xiaowen Zhang, Patrick Lachance, Ankita Dasgupta, and 5 more authors
    The Open Journal of Astrophysics, Feb 2025
    Abs DOI arXiv HTML Code

    Super-resolution is an inverse problem: one low-resolution field maps to many plausible high-resolution realizations. We introduce a deterministic emulator that conditions on high-resolution initial conditions to recover the specific target realization, improving dark matter halo accuracy, merger trees, and Fourier-space cross-correlation against full N-body references.

  3. IJCNLP
    SimAgents: Bridging Literature and the Universe Via A Multi-Agent Large Language Model System
    Xiaowen Zhang, Zhenyu Bi, Patrick LaChance, and 3 more authors
    In Proceedings of the 14th International Joint Conference on Natural Language Processing and the 4th Conference of the Asia-Pacific Chapter of the Association for Computational Linguistics: System Demonstrations, Dec 2025
    Abs DOI arXiv HTML

    SimAgents is a multi-agent LLM system that shortens the loop from reading a paper to running a cosmological simulation: role-specialized agents read literature and user manuals, extract simulation parameters, auto-generate MP-GADGET configurations, and produce first-look analyses, using multi-agent debate and retrieval-augmented generation to control hallucination.
